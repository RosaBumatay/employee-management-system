## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Communication Management Plan is to establish how project information will be shared, documented, and communicated throughout the Employee Management System (EMS) project.

It aims to ensure that stakeholders and project team members receive accurate, relevant, and timely information about project progress, requirements, decisions, risks, issues, and changes.

This plan is a proposed starting point and will be reviewed with the project team once team members and communication preferences have been confirmed.

## 2. Communication Objectives

The project will use communication practices that aim to:

- Keep stakeholders informed about project progress.
- Ensure team members understand their responsibilities and assigned tasks.
- Communicate project decisions, changes, risks, and issues clearly.
- Encourage timely feedback and collaboration.
- Maintain accessible records of important discussions and decisions.
- Reduce misunderstandings and delays caused by incomplete information.

## 3. Stakeholder Communication Requirements

| Stakeholder | Information Required | Proposed Communication |
|---|---|---|
| Project Sponsor | Overall progress, major risks, issues, and decisions requiring approval | Periodic status reports and milestone updates |
| Product Owner | Requirements, backlog priorities, product feedback, and acceptance decisions | Backlog discussions, sprint reviews, and direct updates |
| Project Manager / Coordinator | Schedule, task progress, risks, issues, dependencies, and stakeholder updates | Trello, GitHub, team meetings, and status reports |
| Scrum Master (if assigned) | Team coordination, blockers, and improvement opportunities | Team meetings and direct communication |
| Developers | Requirements, priorities, technical decisions, assigned work, and blockers | Trello, GitHub, and team discussions |
| QA / Testers (if assigned) | Acceptance criteria, test scope, defects, and release readiness | GitHub issues, testing records, and team discussions |
| End Users / Representatives | Requirements clarification, usability feedback, and product demonstrations | Interviews, feedback sessions, and demonstrations |

*Note: These roles and communication needs are proposed. The actual stakeholder list and responsibilities must be validated with the project team.*

## 4. Communication Channels and Tools

The following tools are proposed for project communication and documentation:

| Tool / Channel | Intended Use | Record to Maintain |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub | Project documents, requirements, technical discussions, issues, and code updates | Repository files, issues, and pull requests |
| Trello | Backlog management, task assignments, sprint progress, and blockers | Cards, labels, comments, and board activity |
| Email | Formal stakeholder updates, approvals, and important announcements | Email correspondence and approval records |
| Online Meetings | Sprint planning, progress discussions, reviews, and problem-solving | Meeting notes and action items |
| Team Chat (to be selected) | Quick questions, coordination, and urgent notifications | Important decisions must also be recorded in GitHub or Trello |

The final communication tools will be confirmed with the team. Important project decisions must not remain only in informal chat messages.

## 5. Communication Schedule

The following schedule is a proposed baseline. Meeting frequency and duration will be agreed upon by the team.

| Communication Activity | Proposed Frequency | Participants | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team Check-in | Once per week, or as agreed | Project coordinator and development team | Review progress and identify blockers |
| Sprint Planning | At the beginning of each sprint | Product Owner, Developers, Scrum Master if assigned | Agree on the sprint goal and work the team believes it can complete |
| Sprint Review | At the end of each sprint | Project team and relevant stakeholders | Demonstrate completed work and gather feedback |
| Sprint Retrospective | At the end of each sprint | Scrum Team | Identify improvements to team practices |
| Project Status Report | Weekly | Project coordinator and relevant stakeholders | Summarize progress, risks, issues, and next steps |
| Stakeholder Updates | At agreed milestones or when significant decisions are needed | Project coordinator and relevant stakeholders | Communicate major developments and obtain feedback |
| Urgent Issue Notification | As soon as practical after identification | Relevant team members and decision-makers | Address blockers, security concerns, or major project risks |

## 6. Roles and Communication Responsibilities

| Role | Proposed Communication Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Project Manager / Coordinator | Prepare status reports, maintain communication records, coordinate meetings, and follow up on action items |
| Product Owner | Communicate product goals, clarify requirements, and explain backlog priorities |
| Scrum Master (if assigned) | Support effective team communication and help address collaboration obstacles |
| Developers | Report progress, raise technical concerns, document technical decisions, and communicate blockers |
| QA / Testers (if assigned) | Report defects, testing progress, and quality concerns |
| Project Sponsor | Provide direction and respond to decisions or approvals requiring sponsor involvement |

Responsibilities will be confirmed after the project roles are assigned. The project coordinator does not independently approve technical decisions or product priorities unless that authority is formally assigned.

## 7. Meeting and Reporting Guidelines

To make project communication productive:

1. Share the meeting purpose and agenda in advance when possible.
2. Invite only the stakeholders needed for the discussion.
3. Keep discussions focused on the stated objectives.
4. Record key decisions, action items, owners, and due dates.
5. Share meeting notes with relevant participants.
6. Track assigned actions in Trello or GitHub.
7. Follow up on unresolved issues during the next appropriate check-in.

### Suggested Weekly Status Report Contents

Each weekly status report should include:

- Reporting period and current project phase.
- Completed work and significant progress.
- Work currently in progress.
- Planned next activities.
- Open risks and issues.
- Blockers or decisions requiring attention.
- Changes to scope, schedule, or priorities.
- Items requiring stakeholder feedback or approval.

## 8. Issue Escalation and Response

Issues should be communicated to the appropriate people as early as possible.

| Issue Level | Example | Proposed Action |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Minor clarification or task question | Discuss with the relevant team member and record if needed |
| Medium | Task delay or dependency affecting planned work | Notify the project coordinator and relevant team members |
| High | Major blocker, significant schedule impact, or unresolved requirement | Escalate to the Product Owner and relevant project decision-makers |
| Critical | Serious security concern, data exposure, major system failure, or threat to project viability | Notify the appropriate technical lead and project sponsor immediately through an agreed urgent channel |

The person receiving an escalation should help identify the appropriate owner and next action. Escalation timelines and response expectations will be agreed upon by the team.

## 9. Documentation and Record Keeping

The following records should be maintained in the project repository or project management tools:

- Meeting agendas and minutes.
- Weekly project status reports.
- Important stakeholder feedback.
- Decisions and approval records.
- Requirements clarifications.
- Risk and issue updates.
- Sprint review outcomes.
- Action items and assigned owners.

GitHub will serve as the central repository for formal project documentation. Trello will be used to track work and follow-up actions.

Sensitive information, credentials, and personal employee data must not be placed in public project discussions or repository files.

## 10. Communication Change Management

This plan may be updated when:

- New stakeholders join the project.
- Project roles or responsibilities change.
- The team selects different communication tools.
- Meeting frequency or reporting needs change.
- Communication problems affect project coordination.

Proposed changes should be discussed with the relevant stakeholders. Updates must be documented with a version number, date, and summary of changes.
